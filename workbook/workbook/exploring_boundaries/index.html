Mapping Me

Boundaries as care for access

Boundaries are not only hard lines. They can be pacing, clarity, sensory needs, communication preferences, recovery time, and consent.

The body no

A boundary may show up as tension, delay, avoidance, shutdown, irritation, or a wish to disappear.

Notice: what does your early no feel like?

Access needs are boundaries

Needing clarity, quiet, time, direct language, or fewer transitions can be a boundary, not a preference to apologize for.

Notice: what condition makes participation more possible?

A yes can have shape

Yes might mean yes for one hour, yes with notice, yes by text, yes if I can leave, or yes after I rest.

Notice: what shape would make your yes more honest?

Resentment is a clue

Resentment can point to an overextended yes, an unclear agreement, or a need that has been ignored too long.

Notice: where are you repeatedly giving past capacity?

Boundaries can include repair

A boundary can protect a relationship by making expectations more visible and less dependent on guessing.

Notice: what would be easier if it were named earlier?

Private boundaries count

Not every boundary needs an announcement. Some are choices about pacing, exposure, information, and recovery.

Notice: what boundary could be practiced quietly first?

Prompts for exploring boundaries

These prompts help you notice where a boundary may already exist in your body, behavior, energy, or resentment before it has language.

Capacity boundary

Where do you often say yes before checking whether you have enough capacity?

Communication boundary

What communication conditions help you stay clear, honest, and less overloaded?

Boundary script

What is a sentence you could try that is specific, kind enough, and not over-explained?

Aftercare

After a boundary is set, what support might help your body believe it was allowed?
