Mapping Me

Small clues about what matters

Values are not homework answers. They often show up as irritation, relief, admiration, loyalty, resistance, or the tiny things you keep returning to.

Friction can be a clue

When something feels wrong, it may be pointing toward a value that is being crowded, rushed, or ignored.

Notice: what kind of situation makes you quietly tense?

Relief has information

A moment of relief can show what your nervous system, attention, or sense of self has been needing.

Notice: what makes your shoulders drop a little?

What you protect

The things you defend, save, repair, or make room for often reveal values before you can name them.

Notice: what do you keep making space for?

Admiration points somewhere

People, places, tools, and communities you admire may carry qualities you want near your own life.

Notice: what do you admire without needing to become it?

Cost matters

A value is allowed to be real even when living it costs energy. The cost is information, not failure.

Notice: which values need support, pacing, or boundaries?

What matters can shift

Some values stay steady. Others become more important in certain seasons, relationships, bodies, or environments.

Notice: what matters more right now than it used to?
