Mapping Me

Emotions as information, not instructions

An emotion can be real without being the whole story. This workbook is for making space around feelings before deciding what they mean.

Name it lightly

A feeling name can be a temporary label, not a final diagnosis of the moment.

Notice: does the label make more room or make the feeling tighter?

Mixed feelings count

Relief and grief, anger and care, excitement and dread can sit in the same room.

Notice: which two feelings are both asking to be believed?

The body may know first

Emotion can arrive as heat, pressure, restlessness, tears, blankness, or a sudden need to leave.

Notice: where did the feeling show up before it had words?

Aftershocks are real

Some emotions arrive after the event, once there is enough safety or quiet to feel them.

Notice: what are you feeling later that you could not feel then?

Signal, not command

A feeling may point toward a need, boundary, loss, value, or fear. It does not have to choose your action alone.

Notice: what might this feeling be protecting or requesting?

Small response

Processing can be a glass of water, a note, a pause, a message draft, a walk, or letting the feeling be witnessed.

Notice: what would help the feeling move one inch?

Mapping Me

Prompts for emotional processing

Use these prompts slowly. The aim is not to fix the feeling quickly; it is to notice its shape, context, message, and possible next support.

Feeling shape

If this emotion had a shape, texture, speed, color, or temperature, what would it be?

Before, during, after

What was happening before the feeling appeared, while it was strongest, and after it shifted?

Need underneath

What need might be sitting underneath this feeling, even if the need is not easy to meet right now?

Safe expression

What is one way this feeling could be expressed without turning into harm, pressure, or performance?
