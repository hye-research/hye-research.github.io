Sensory systems

Exploring different sensory channels

This is not a test or diagnosis. It is a way to notice which inputs cost energy, which ones help, and which ones depend on context.

Visual

Light, color, patterns, movement, and visual clutter.

Screens, fluorescent lights, busy rooms, tiny details, sudden movement.

Auditory

Sound volume, tone, rhythm, background noise, and surprise sounds.

Alarms, overlapping voices, humming appliances, music, silence.

Tactile

Touch, texture, pressure, temperature, clothing, and skin sensations.

Tags, seams, hair, hugs, sticky hands, heat, cold, light touch.

Vestibular

Balance, spinning, swinging, speed, and head position.

Car rides, escalators, swings, leaning back, quick turns, dizziness.

Proprioceptive

Body position, pressure, weight, force, and muscle feedback.

Pushing, carrying, stretching, pressure, bumping into things.

Interoceptive

Inside-body signals like hunger, thirst, pain, heartbeat, and fatigue.

Forgetting to eat, noticing pain late, sudden overwhelm, body check-ins.

Taste & smell

Flavors, food textures, smells, nausea, and scent memories.

Perfume, cooking smells, toothpaste, food texture, aftertaste.

Supports

Things to notice and try

These are starting points, not rules. The useful question is: does this make the environment easier to be in?

Visual

Bright or flickering light can drain energy before it feels obvious.

  • Softer lighting
  • A less busy desk
  • Screen brightness that feels easier
  • One clear focus area

Auditory

Layered sound can make simple tasks feel much harder.

  • Quieter background sound
  • A short quiet break
  • Clear warning before loud sounds
  • Captions when helpful

Tactile

Small tactile irritations can become big when tired or stressed.

  • Clothes that feel comfortable
  • Fewer irritating textures
  • A preferred blanket or layer
  • A backup outfit for long days

Vestibular

Balance input can be calming for some people and nauseating for others.

  • Slower transitions
  • A stable seat
  • Gentle movement breaks
  • Warning before sudden movement

Proprioceptive

Body feedback can help when thoughts feel scattered or floaty.

  • A short walk
  • Stretching in a comfortable way
  • Holding something grounding
  • Tasks that use the body gently

Interoceptive

Body signals may arrive late, quietly, or all at once.

  • Simple body check-ins
  • Water and snack reminders
  • Noticing pain or fatigue earlier
  • A personal energy scale

Taste & smell

Smell and taste can strongly affect focus, nausea, and mood.

  • Lower-scent spaces
  • Reliable foods
  • Texture choices that feel okay
  • Fresh air when smells build up
